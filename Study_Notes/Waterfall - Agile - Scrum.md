#### **Software Process Models and Agile Introduction (3.a)**

##### **Software Process:**

- A structured set of activities required to develop software, including:
    
    - **Specification**: Defining system requirements.
        
    - **Design and Implementation**: Organizing and building the system.
        
    - **Validation**: Ensuring the system meets customer needs.
        
    - **Evolution**: Adapting to changing requirements.
        

##### **Plan-Driven vs. Agile Processes:**

- **Plan-Driven:**
    
    - Detailed advance planning.
        
    - Progress measured against the plan.
        
    - Suited for projects with well-defined requirements.
        
- **Agile:**
    
    - Incremental planning for adaptability.
        
    - Combines flexibility with practical elements of plan-driven processes.
        
---
##### **Waterfall Model:**

- Plan-driven, sequential phases of specification and development.
    
- Benefits:
    
    - Effective for large, stable, and well-defined projects.
        
- Drawbacks:
    
    - Inflexible to requirement changes.
        
    - Unsuitable for systems with evolving needs.
        

##### **Incremental Development:**

- Specification, development, and validation occur in overlapping cycles.
    
- Benefits:
    
    - Reduced cost of adapting to changes.
        
    - Enables rapid feedback and earlier delivery.
        
- Drawbacks:
    
    - Less visibility for managers.
        
    - Risk of system structure degradation.
        
---
##### **Agile Software Engineering:**

- Emphasizes rapid delivery, adaptability, and reduced overhead.
    
- Originated as a response to limitations of plan-driven models.
    
- Prioritizes:
    
    - Individuals and interactions over processes and tools.
        
    - Working software over documentation.
        
    - Customer collaboration over contracts.
        
    - Flexibility over rigid planning.
        

##### **Agile Manifesto:**

- Values include individuals, interactions, working software, collaboration, and responsiveness to change.
    

##### **Agile Principles:**

- Close customer involvement.
    
- Embracing change.
    
- Incremental development.
    
- Simplicity in processes.
    
- Trusting and empowering the development team.
    
---
##### **Extreme Programming (XP):**

- Pushes best practices to extreme levels.
    
- Key practices:
    
    - **Incremental Planning**: Uses prioritized user stories.
        
    - **Small Releases**: Frequent minimal functionality updates.
        
    - **Test-Driven Development**: Writing tests before code.
        
    - **Continuous Integration**: Regularly integrating and testing code.
        
    - **Refactoring**: Enhancing code quality and maintainability.
        

##### **Situational Appropriateness:**

- **Waterfall:** Critical, stable, and complex systems.
    
- **Agile:** Dynamic projects with iterative needs.
    
- **XP:** Projects with focus on rapid feedback and continuous testing.

#### **Agile Part II: Scrum (3.b)**

##### **Scrum Overview:**

- Popular agile framework for project management.
    
- Focuses on planning and organization without prescribing technical practices.
    

##### **Scrum Framework Components:**

1. **Scrum Team:**
    
    - **Product Owner**: Manages backlog and ensures alignment with goals.
        
    - **ScrumMaster**: Facilitates Scrum practices; acts as a coach.
        
    - **Developers**: Cross-functional team responsible for product increments.
        
2. **Events:**
    
    - **Sprint**: A timeboxed cycle (up to one month) for developing increments.
        
    - **Sprint Planning**: Defines goals, selects backlog items, and plans tasks.
        
    - **Daily Scrum**: Short daily meeting to discuss progress and plans.
        
    - **Sprint Review**: Stakeholder meeting to inspect outcomes.
        
    - **Sprint Retrospective**: Identifies improvements for future sprints.
        
3. **Artifacts:**
    
    - **Product Backlog**: Prioritized list of features, tasks, and improvements.
        
    - **Sprint Backlog**: Detailed plan for the current sprint.
        
    - **Increment**: Usable product output from a sprint.
        

##### **Scrum Pillars:**

- Transparency, Inspection, and Adaptation.
    

##### **Scrum Values:**

- Commitment, Focus, Openness, Respect, and Courage.
    

##### **Key Scrum Practices:**

- **Timeboxed Sprints:** Fixed durations for focused work.
    
- **Self-Organizing Teams:** Autonomous decision-making.
    
- **Cross-Functional Teams:** Diverse skills within the team.
    

##### **Product Backlog:**

- Includes features, user requests, development activities, and improvements.
    
- Activities:
    
    - **Creation**: Adding new items.
        
    - **Refinement**: Detailing and prioritizing items.
        
    - **Estimation**: Assessing effort using metrics like story points.
        

##### **Sprint Activities:**

- Establish goals, plan tasks, and update the sprint backlog.
    
- Conduct daily standups to track progress.
    
- Review and retrospect for continuous improvement.
    

##### **Optional Practices:**

- Test Automation and Continuous Integration for efficient and reliable development.
    
- Code Completeness Checklist:
    
    - Code review, unit tests, integration, and acceptance criteria.
        

##### **Team Size and Management:**

- Ideal team size: ≤8 members.
    
- Diverse skills for effective communication and resilience.
    
- ScrumMaster often handles reporting and project management.
