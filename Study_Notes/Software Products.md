# Software Products

- **Software products are generic software systems that are useful to a range of customers.** Examples include large-scale business systems, personal products, mobile phone apps and games. **Software product engineering methods stemmed from techniques used to support the development of custom software systems.**

---
- **Custom (Project-based) software systems are developed in dedicated software projects.** Development begins with a set of software requirements provided by an external client that outline what the software system should do. The software company designs and implements the system to meet the requirements. The customer can change the requirements at any time. Custom software usually has a long lifetime (10 years or more) and must be supported (by a support team).
---

- **Product software development starts with a business opportunity.** A company will develop a software product to take advantage of the opportunity. The software development company decides on the development timescale, features to include and when the product should change. Rapid delivery is essential to capture the market.
---

- A **software product line is a set of software products that share a common core**. Each product line includes customer-specific adaptations and additions. Software product lines can be used to implement a custom system that cannot be met by a generic product.

---
- A **platform is a software (or software + hardware) product that allows new applications to be built on it**.

---
- In **product development, there is no external customer that generates requirements and pays for the software.** This is also true for other types of software development, such as student projects, research software and internal tool development.
---

- **Software product management is a business activity that focuses on software products developed and sold by the business**. Product managers have overall responsibility for the product and are involved (not mainly focused) in planning, development and marketing. They are the interface between the organization, its customers, and the software development team. Product managers must focus on customers and potential customers.

---
- **Product managers have several concerns**, including:
    
    - **Business Needs**: Ensuring the developed software meets the business goals of the development company.
    - **Technology Constraints**: Making developers aware of technology issues.
    - **Customer Experience**: Understanding customer needs and how they use the product.
---

- **Product managers have many technical interactions** including:
    
    - Product Vision Management: Helping to develop and manage the product vision and prevent "vision drift".
    - Product Roadmap Development: Leading the plan's progress including the development, release and marketing of the software.
    - User Story and Scenario Development: Using user stories and scenarios to refine a product vision and identify product features.
    - Product Backlog Creation and Management: Creating and refining the prioritized "to-do" list of what needs to be developed.
    - Acceptance Testing: Verifying that a software release meets the goals set out in the roadmap and that the product is efficient and reliable.
    - Customer Testing: Getting feedback from customers on the product's features, usability, and business value.

---
- **Product prototyping is the process of developing an early version of a product to test ideas and demonstrate market potential.** Prototypes help users relate to a product, point out what they like and do not like, and make suggestions for new features. Prototypes can also help identify fundamental software components and test technology.
    
---

- **Two-stage prototyping** involves:
    
    - **Feasibility Demonstration**: Creating an executable system that demonstrates the new ideas in your product. This helps determine if the ideas work and showcase the product's features.
    - **Customer Demonstration**: Extending an existing prototype based on user feedback and ensure that the product meets the needs of its target audience.
---
- **Key takeaways**:
    
    - Software products include functionality useful to a wide range of customers.
    - In product software engineering, the same company decides on the features and implementation.
    - Software products can be stand-alone, hybrid, or service-based systems.
    - Key responsibilities of product managers include product vision ownership, product roadmap development, user stories, product backlog, and customer and acceptance testing.
    - Product managers work between the business, development team and customers.
    - Developing a product prototype helps refine ideas and demonstrate features to potential customers.
